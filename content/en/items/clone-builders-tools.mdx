The HUD strip in Clone Builders looks simple, but the clone builders tools packed into it decide whether a big project feels smooth or falls apart. Saves, the Tools/Edit button, minigames, Speed, and Noclip are not cosmetic extras — they are the controls that stop a sprawling construction from becoming a mess. Learning the clone builders tools early means fewer lost builds, faster edits, and a finished creation you can actually share with other players.

This guide walks through each tool in the clone builders tools menu, explains what it stores or changes, and shows the moments when you should reach for it.

## Clone Builders Tools at a Glance

Before digging into individual controls, it helps to see the whole toolset side by side. Each entry below lives on or near the HUD and serves a different phase of a project — from assembling parts to showing off the result.

| Tool | Where It Lives | Primary Function | Reach For It When |
|---|---|---|---|
| Saves | HUD save control | Stores completed projects so they survive future game updates | A build just hit 100% |
| Tools / Edit | HUD button beside the other controls | Opens editing so you can adjust builds and crew behavior | Parts, placement, or crew assignments need changes |
| Minigame | Finished-build menu | Converts a completed structure into a playable prompt | You want other players to interact with your build |
| Speed | HUD movement tool | Increases your movement rate across the map | Walking between distant build sites |
| Noclip | HUD movement tool | Lets you pass through geometry | Inspecting interiors or tight gaps |

The order matters. Save is a finishing tool, Edit is a maintenance tool, Minigame is a publishing tool, and Speed plus Noclip are inspection tools. Players who treat them all as "options menus" tend to lose work they did not realize was unsaved, or spend ten minutes walking when thirty seconds of Speed would have done it.

## Saves: The Tool That Protects Finished Work

The Saves control is the least exciting entry in the clone builders tools list and the one you will regret ignoring. According to community reports, its job is to hold onto completed projects so that a game update does not quietly erase a structure you spent hours designing. That makes it a finishing step, not a mid-build one.

A practical save routine looks like this:

| Step | Action | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Confirm the build reads 100% and the piece count has stopped climbing | A save taken mid-assembly may store an incomplete state |
| 2 | Open the Saves control from the HUD | You want the completed project registered, not just on screen |
| 3 | Verify the project appears in your saved list | A silent failure is worse than an obvious one |
| 4 | Re-check after a game update | Updates are the main threat to unsaved projects |

Player experience suggests that saving after every milestone — not only at full completion — is the safer habit. If you have ever watched a build vanish after a patch, you already know why. The tool is fast; the rebuild is not.

## Editing Tools and the Tools/Edit Button

The Tools/Edit button opens the editing layer for your builds and your crew. It sits alongside neighboring HUD controls, and each one touches a different part of the system — some affect the structure, others affect the clones doing the work.

| Editing Area | What You Can Adjust | Typical Reason to Open It |
|---|---|---|
| Build placement | Where the structure sits and how it fits the terrain | The location feels cramped or obstructed |
| Assembly details | Individual parts and their arrangement | A section came out wrong |
| Crew assignment | Which clones are tied to the project | Progress has stalled or feels unbalanced |
| Build queue behavior | Whether a build waits or proceeds | You are juggling several projects at once |

Editing is also where you fix the most common frustration in the game: a build that stops progressing because the crew is stretched too thin. Community reports point out that a stalled queue usually traces back to crew status rather than a broken build. Opening the edit controls and reading each clone's current activity answers that question faster than waiting to see if it resolves itself.

Because editing touches both structures and clones, it pairs naturally with `More Clones` and `Assign Clones` upgrades. If you keep expanding crew capacity without revisiting assignments, you end up with idle clones and a queue that still crawls.

## Speed Control and Noclip: Moving Around Large Projects

Once a construction gets genuinely big, traversal becomes the real time sink. Speed and Noclip exist to fix that, and they solve different problems.

| Tool | What It Changes | Best Use Case | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Speed | Your movement rate | Crossing the map between distant build sites | A faster speed can make precise part placement trickier |
| Noclip | Collision with geometry | Looking inside walls, under floors, behind facades | You can end up positioned inside solid geometry if you are careless |

The two work best as a pair. Switch on Speed to cover ground, then swap to Noclip when you arrive and need to get inside a finished shell. Camera angle matters as much as movement here — pulling the camera back and orbiting slowly reveals gaps and misaligned pieces that a straight-on view hides completely.

Players who build large multi-part structures often report that inspection is where Noclip earns its place in the clone builders tools lineup. It is far cheaper to spot a mistake during a fly-through than to disassemble a section after the build has been finalized.

## Minigames: Turning a Build Into Something Playable

This is the tool that changes what a build *is*. Rather than leaving a finished structure as decoration, the minigame option turns it into a playable experience with a prompt, hosted on a private server. That turns a construction project into something other players can actually engage with.

| Step | What Happens | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Finish the build completely | A minigame built on an unfinished structure plays badly |
| 2 | Open the minigame option from the finished build | It only appears meaningfully once the project is done |
| 3 | Write your prompt | Keep it short and specific — this is the player's instruction |
| 4 | Host on a private server | Gives you a controlled space to test and share |
| 5 | Run it yourself first | Catch a confusing prompt before friends do |

The prompt is the part most players get wrong. A vague instruction leaves testers wandering with no idea what to do. A tight one — reach a target, survive a route, complete a sequence — makes the same structure feel designed rather than improvised. Since the host controls the private server, you can iterate on the prompt without an audience watching you fix it in real time.

## Practical Tips for Getting More From Clone Builders Tools

Beyond the basics, a few habits separate players who build comfortably from players who fight the interface.

- **Save before you experiment.** Any time you are about to test a risky edit, save the current state first.
- **Edit in passes.** Fix placement in one session and crew assignments in another rather than doing everything at once.
- **Use Speed for travel, Noclip for inspection.** Mixing them up wastes time — Noclip is a poor way to cross a map.
- **Build the minigame prompt around a single goal.** One clear objective beats three vague ones.
- **Revisit crew status whenever a queue stalls.** The bottleneck is almost never the structure itself.

Common mistakes show up again and again in discussions of the clone builders tools system:

| Mistake | What It Costs You | The Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Never using Saves until the end | Losing an entire project to an update | Save at every milestone |
| Editing crew and structure in the same pass | Confusion about what actually changed | Separate your edit sessions |
| Building a minigame on an unfinished structure | A broken, unplayable result | Wait for 100% completion |
| Using Noclip as a travel tool | Getting stuck inside geometry | Travel with Speed, inspect with Noclip |

## Why the Tool Order Matters Over a Long Session

A typical long session moves through the tools in a predictable sequence. You place a build, watch clones assemble it, check progress percentage and piece counts, edit what went wrong, and then — only once everything reads complete — save and convert it into a minigame. Skipping a step rarely breaks anything immediately, but it compounds.

| Session Phase | Tool You Should Be Using | What You Are Checking |
|---|---|---|
| Setup | Edit controls | Placement and available crew |
| Assembly | Edit + crew status | Queue movement and clone activity |
| Review | Speed + Noclip | Alignment, gaps, interior details |
| Finalize | Saves | Completed project secured |
| Publish | Minigame | Prompt and private server hosting |

The players who finish the most projects are usually not the ones with the biggest crews — they are the ones who follow this order instead of jumping straight from assembly to publishing. Saving late and editing early are the two habits that cost the most time.

## FAQ

### What are the main clone builders tools on the HUD?

The core set is Saves, the Tools/Edit button, the minigame option for finished builds, and the Speed and Noclip movement controls. Together they cover finishing, editing, publishing, and inspecting. Most other HUD controls sit next to these and handle related crew or build functions.

### Do clone builders tools save my work automatically?

Community reports suggest you should not assume automatic saving. The Saves control exists precisely so you can secure completed projects, particularly around game updates. Treating it as a manual finishing step is the safer approach, and saving at each milestone protects you further.

### Can I turn any build into a minigame?

The minigame option is intended for finished builds, and it works best when you create a clear prompt and host the result on a private server. Trying to convert a partially assembled structure usually produces a poor experience, so wait until the build reads fully complete.

### Are Speed and Noclip safe to use in normal play?

They are standard movement tools, but each has a tradeoff. Speed makes precise placement harder, and Noclip can leave you positioned inside solid geometry. Use Speed to travel and Noclip to inspect, and you will get the benefit without the downside.

Getting comfortable with the clone builders tools is really about sequencing: edit first, inspect second, save third, publish last. Do that consistently and even large sandbox projects stay manageable.
